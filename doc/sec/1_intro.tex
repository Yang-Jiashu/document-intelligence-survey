\section{Introduction}

Document intelligence must connect what a page shows with what an answer claims. A financial question may require a table cell, its year and unit, and a correction on another page. An answer can fail at any link: character recognition, row--column alignment, page retrieval or calculation. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline_overview} follows these links from a document to a source-backed answer.

\subsection{Representations and Evidence}

OCR-based systems expose text and coordinates; visual models encode page appearance; hybrid systems combine both. These interfaces preserve different information. OCR may miss a symbol or its reading order, while image resizing may erase fine print. Retrieval decides which evidence reaches the reader, and tools can make numerical operations explicit. A system may combine all of these mechanisms.

We compare methods through five questions: \textbf{representation} (text, coordinates or pixels), \textbf{context} (region, page or collection), \textbf{granularity} (token, cell or page), \textbf{control} (fixed or iterative access), and \textbf{validation} (answer, source or computation). Table understanding is a task family, RAG an access strategy and a VLM a model family; they are compatible choices rather than competing categories.

\paragraph{One question, two pages.}
Figure~\ref{fig:cross_page_case} shows the running example. A table gives margins of 15\% and 12\%, but a separate note restates the earlier profit from 96 to 104. With revenue unchanged at 800, the corrected margin is 13\%: the increase is 2 percentage points. Retrieving the table alone gives a plausible but incorrect 3-point answer. Executing subtraction cannot repair the missing note.

\subsection{Scope and Reading Map}

This curated narrative review covers document layout, OCR, table reasoning, retrieval, visual models and evaluation. Existing surveys organize LLM-based document AI~\cite{wang2025documentintelligencesurvey} and multimodal document RAG~\cite{gao2025scalingbeyondcontext}; our focus is how representations and inference choices preserve, select and verify evidence. Representative mechanisms appear in the main text, with the broader literature retained in the project's searchable reading catalog.

Figure~\ref{fig:document_ai_chronology} provides a chronological entry point. Dates refer to first public papers; yellow highlights mark official trained weights verified on 9 November 2026. The chronology records coexistence rather than a ranking or replacement of earlier methods. This survey synthesizes published designs and protocols without introducing a new model benchmark.
