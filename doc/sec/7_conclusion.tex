\section{Research Priorities and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Three research priorities follow from the interfaces examined in this survey.

\paragraph{Preserve evidence across conversions.}
Text, coordinates, cells and image patches should retain a reversible link to their source page. In the running example, a number without its year or restatement is insufficient. Future systems should expose what is discarded during serialization, cropping and token compression, and test whether an answer changes when this information is restored.

\paragraph{Spend computation where evidence is missing.}
A clean table may need only a reader and calculator; a small footnote may need a high-resolution crop; a cross-document question may need retrieval. Compare fixed context, one-shot retrieval and iterative access under the same source and inference budget. Additional tool calls are useful when they recover evidence or correct an error, rather than repeat a plan.

\paragraph{Evaluate the complete support chain.}
Measure answer accuracy, evidence localization, source support and execution separately. Include cases with missing evidence, distracting pages, degraded scans and changing document collections. Evidence-removal or substitution controls can test reliance on a claimed source, while accuracy--coverage curves reveal whether abstention is useful.

Document intelligence therefore requires more than fluent answers or accurate OCR in isolation. The practical design question is which representation and access pattern preserve the evidence required by a task. The visual workflows and mechanism comparisons in this survey make that question explicit across perception, retrieval, table computation and multimodal reasoning.
