\abstract{%
Document intelligence connects the recognition of page content with reasoning over the evidence that documents contain. Its central difficulty is preserving useful information across representations: OCR can lose characters and reading order, table serialization can obscure cell relationships, visual compression can discard fine detail, and retrieval can omit evidence needed for an answer. This survey examines these interfaces across layout analysis, OCR, table understanding, text-based retrieval-augmented generation, and vision-language models for document question answering.

We organize representative approaches using five complementary axes: input representation, context span, evidence granularity, inference control, and validation. These axes expose combinations that a task-only or model-only taxonomy can obscure, including OCR-assisted visual systems, multimodal retrieval, and tool-assisted table reasoning. Within each area, we compare the bottlenecks addressed by different mechanisms, the costs they introduce, and the conditions under which their advantages may fail to transfer. We distinguish reported benchmark results from new experimental evidence and discuss the input access, model versions, retrieval settings, and computational budgets required for meaningful comparisons. Finally, we separate answer accuracy, evidence localization, evidence support, and execution correctness to formulate concrete evaluation needs. The resulting perspective treats modular, OCR-free, and agentic systems as complementary design choices and identifies evidence preservation and verification as a common research agenda.
}
